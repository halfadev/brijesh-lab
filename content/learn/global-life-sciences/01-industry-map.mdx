There is no single company called “the pharmaceutical industry.” What looks like one industry from the outside is a network of organizations performing very different jobs.

Some discover medicines. Some fund or develop them. Some own the legal right to sell them. Some manufacture them. Others store, distribute, prescribe, dispense, administer, reimburse, regulate, or track them.

<KeyConcept>
  Who owns it, who makes it, who has it, who moves it, who pays for it, and who regulates it can all be different organizations.
</KeyConcept>

## Start with the patient

A patient needs a medicine or therapy. Everything upstream exists to discover, develop, manufacture, approve, finance, deliver, and monitor that treatment.

Then move backward through the network.

## The major actor groups

### Innovator pharma and biotech

These organizations own or develop drug assets, clinical programs, intellectual property, regulatory dossiers, and eventually commercial products.

### Manufacturing organizations

Internal plants and CMO/CDMO partners create drug substance, finished product, fill-finish operations, and packaging.

### Distribution and care delivery

3PLs store and fulfill product. Wholesalers buy and redistribute it. Health systems, clinics, and pharmacies receive the medicine closer to the patient and ultimately dispense or administer it.

### Economic and regulatory actors

Payers, PBMs, and GPOs influence access, contracting, reimbursement, and formulary economics. Regulators define the legal requirements for approval, quality, safety, traceability, and market participation.

## Three flows run through the system

The **drug** moves physically. The **data** describes identity, custody, events, orders, shipment, and receipt. The **dollar** moves through purchase, reimbursement, contracting, rebates, credits, and chargebacks.
